# Representative Log and Roundtable — Examples

The daily log below is a format-faithful ILLUSTRATIVE example,
constructed to show every label, evidence tag, correction, and dispute
pattern from the Log Standard. It is NOT a verbatim copy of any real
private log, and its entries must not be cited as real events. Amounts,
identifiers, and private details are omitted or shown as `[redacted]`.

The roundtable example is likewise illustrative: it shows the SHAPE of
a completed roundtable once all answers are in. The seat lines are NOT
real quotes.

A note on what is not here: the source pack also contained a sanitized
snapshot of a real, still-collecting roundtable from the original
panel. It is excluded from this public edition because its topic was
the owner's own professional identity, which does not generalize; the
illustrative completed roundtable below teaches the same format. See
PROVENANCE.md.

## Part 1 — Representative Daily Log (illustrative)

```
Seat 2 Daily Log — 2026-10
Header:
- Covers: First-hand Seat 2 sessions with the owner, Oct 2026.
- Sources / channels: Seat 2 chat sessions; shared panel folder (read for format only).
- Updated: By Seat 2 directly in the shared folder when the write path works; otherwise the owner pastes entries.

Monday, October 12, 2026

JOB SEARCH
1. Discussed: Reviewed a remote strategy-role listing with the owner; noted it was IT / technology strategy, not generalist corporate strategy. (10:04 AM ET, Seat 2 chat). [Owner said]
2. Decided: The owner will not pursue listings that require weekly onsite work. Standing. (10:11 AM ET, Seat 2 chat). [Owner said]
3. Completed: Drafted a tailored resume bullet for the owner's review; nothing submitted. (10:32 AM ET, Seat 2 chat). [Verified: draft shown in chat, no submission made]

DOMAIN / IDENTITY
4. Discussed: Compared two domain options for a personal professional identity; noted renewal-cost and longevity tradeoffs. (11:02 AM ET, Seat 2 chat). [Unverified]
5. Open: Awaiting the owner — whether to hold a second domain as defensive insurance. (11:05 AM ET, Seat 2 chat).

PANEL / LOGGING
6. Handoff: From the owner → Seat 2, question on domain setup, status answered in this log. (11:10 AM ET, Seat 2 chat).
7. Corrected: Entry #4 above said one option was "[redacted claim]"; correct is "[redacted correction]". Found by re-reading the registrar page. (11:47 AM ET, registrar page). [Verified: re-read the live page]
8. Disputed: Disagree with Seat 3, Oct 12, #9 on the renewal-cost figure; registrar page shows a different figure. Detail in Seat 2 Cross-Read, Oct 12. (12:03 PM ET, registrar page). [Verified: re-read the live page]

For Current State: None. (No new open items; correction in #7 does not change Current State.)

Footer: Entries: 8. Coverage: Seat 2 chats only; did not read other seats' logs today except as noted. Section 9 exclusions applied — no credentials / codes / full account numbers; no health / legal identifiers.
Optional Usage line: [Omit if platform does not expose usage; do not invent a figure.]
```

### What this example demonstrates

- **Evidence:** Every Completed entry has a tag. “Resolved / fixed / sent” would need `[Verified: how]`. A draft is not a sent message.
- **Decisions:** A Decided entry records the owner's choice, with “Standing” when it applies going forward.
- **Corrections:** Appended, never rewritten. A Corrected entry names the entry, what it said, what is true, and how the error was found. The original entry is left in place with an optional pointer: `(corrected, see Oct 12 #7)`.
- **Disagreements:** A Disputed entry cites the other log’s entry number + your evidence, and points to the Cross-Read detail. It does not silently overwrite the other log, and it does not go into “For Current State” from the Cross-Read.
- **Handoffs:** The receiving assistant logs the answer; the sending assistant does not record the answer as fact.
- **End of day:** “For Current State” proposals (Add / Change / Close + entry number) or “None”. Footer with count, coverage, and Section 9 confirmation.
- **First-hand rule:** No open items, preferences, completed actions, or facts copied from another log. The only cross-log line allowed in the Daily Log is a one-line “Read:” note or a Disputed pointer.

## Part 2 — Illustrative completed roundtable (NOT real quotes)

The example below shows the SHAPE of a completed roundtable once all answers are in. Topic and panelist lines are illustrative. Do not treat them as what any panelist actually said.

```
Roundtable: Example — Choosing a weekly review format — 2026-10-14
Status: Synthesized

Question: What weekly review format should the panel use so the owner sees open items once, without duplicate nags?

Seat 1, the Coordinator (illustrative):
Recommendation: One Sunday review + one midweek check, no daily nags.
Reasoning: The owner already has daily logs; a weekly roll-up prevents duplicate reminders.
Risks: Items can go stale between reviews; needs a "Stale?" rule.
What I would change about the framing: Add a deadline rule for stale items.
Confidence: Medium — depends on whether the 6:30 AM reconciliation stays reliable.

Seat 4 (illustrative):
Recommendation: Same cadence, plus a short "Blocked only" midweek list.
Reasoning: Blocked items need faster surfacing than open items.
Risks: Two lists can diverge.
Confidence: Medium.

Seat 5 (illustrative — pasted):
Recommendation: Weekly only; no midweek check.
Reasoning: Fewer interruptions outweigh faster blocked-item surfacing.
Risks: Blocked items wait up to a week.
Confidence: Low — would change if a blocked item missed a deadline.

Seat 2 (illustrative):
Recommendation: Sunday review + midweek "Blocked only" list, with stale-item rule.
Reasoning: Matches Seat 1 + Seat 4 on cadence; adopts Seat 5's concern by keeping the midweek list to blocked items only.
Risks: Needs a clear definition of "blocked" or the midweek list creeps.
What I would change about the framing: Define "blocked" vs "open" in the template.
Confidence: Medium.

Seat 3 (illustrative):
Recommendation: Sunday review + midweek "Blocked only" list.
Reasoning: Same as Seat 2; the stale-item rule covers Seat 5's risk.
Risks: Checker workload.
Confidence: Medium.

Synthesis by Seat 1, the Coordinator (illustrative):
Where they agree: Sunday review; no daily nags; a stale-item rule is needed.
Where they disagree: Midweek check — Seat 5 prefers weekly-only; others prefer a midweek "Blocked only" list.
Gaps — what no one addressed: Who owns sending the midweek list if the Coordinator is unavailable.
Recommendation: Adopt Sunday review + midweek "Blocked only" list + 14-day "Stale?" rule; define "blocked" in the template; name a backup sender.

Decision (illustrative):
The owner's final call: Adopt the synthesis recommendation.
Date decided: [date]
Reason, in the owner's words: [the owner's words, verbatim]
Follow-ups: [Next step — owner — date]
```

### What the completed example demonstrates

- Each panelist writes only in its own section, with recommendation / reasoning / risks / framing challenge / confidence.
- Disagreement is recorded explicitly (“Where they disagree”), not smoothed over. Gaps are named (“what no one addressed”).
- Synthesis is the Coordinator's job, after all answers are in. Seats do not write the Synthesis or Decision sections.
- The Decision records the owner's final call in the owner's own words, with date + follow-ups. Pasted answers are marked as pasted and kept verbatim.
- No credentials, codes, full account numbers, health details, or legal identifiers appear in any section.
